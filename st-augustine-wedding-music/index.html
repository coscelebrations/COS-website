The part of a St. Augustine wedding most people wing is the ceremony sound. COS Celebrations runs every ceremony on a small-footprint satellite speaker, an omnidirectional lapel mic that picks up both of you during the vows, a wireless handheld for the officiant and readers, and a second lapel when someone speaks softly. Guests tell us almost every weekend it is the best ceremony sound they have heard.

Ceremony

The Part Everyone Overlooks

A lot of people wing the ceremony with average gear. Not us. We take a ton of pride in ceremony sound, and it starts with a satellite speaker small enough to set up in any of the odd-shaped spaces downtown.

The mic default is an omnidirectional lapel on the officiant. It's a 360-degree microphone, so it picks up both of you for the I do's and the vows. Officiants who prefer a handheld get one, and a handheld on a stand off to the side covers the friend reading a poem or scripture.

Writing your own vows and you talk quietly? We either have the officiant hold the handheld close, or we clip a second lapel on you so every row hears it.

You get at least 30 minutes of prelude music before start time while guests arrive. And the ceremony itself does not run off Spotify. It runs on real DJ software, so if you want a section looped, a lyric cut, or the kiss landing on a specific part of the song, that's exactly what happens.

Cocktail Hour

Live Music That Still Lets People Talk

Cocktail hour gets its own satellite speaker too, tucked out of the way. Almost everything we run is professional battery-powered sound, and the pieces that aren't plug into our own battery packs. That means a saxophonist, a keys player, or a musician running tracks can set up anywhere the venue lets us stand, including the spots with no outlet within fifty feet.

30 min of prelude music, guaranteed, before every ceremony
2 lapels when the vows are soft-spoken, so every row hears them
10 PM outdoor music cutoff downtown. Inside runs later.

Reception

There Is No Number One Wedding Song

Couples ask. The honest answer: no such thing. There are classics that still land with every age in the room, and we use them to open the floor. Celebration. September. Ain't No Mountain High Enough. Dancing Queen. Stayin' Alive. Get Down On It.

After that it's reading the room, which is the actual job. The St. Augustine wedding DJ page covers the reception in full. When the DJ is joined by live sax and drums, that's Pulse!, our hybrid band.

What live music adds to the price depends on three things: how many musicians, which part of the day, and how long you want them. A DJ is $1,500. DJ plus sax runs $2,000 to $4,500. Pulse! starts at $3,500. The live musicians page covers who plays what, and the wedding saxophonist page covers the sax specifically.

Questions

St. Augustine Wedding Music, Answered

Where can I find live music for a wedding in St. Augustine?

We bring it to you. Sax, violin, keys, drums, or the full Pulse! hybrid band, set up anywhere from the Fountain of Youth patio to the Lightner courtyard on battery power. You are not limited to a venue's house band.

Do you play ceremony music from Spotify?

No. Ceremonies run on professional DJ software so we can loop a section, cut a lyric, or land the kiss on the exact bar you want. Streaming apps cannot do that, and they drop out.

What time does wedding music have to stop in downtown St. Augustine?

Outdoor music downtown ends at 10 PM. Move inside and most venues run later: the Lightner usually wraps around 11:30, the Treasury sets it by your package. Always check your contract.

Where does the after-party go in St. Augustine?

Prohibition Kitchen is the big one, with a band from 10 to 1 on weekends. Tradewinds, Barley Republic, Ann O'Malley's, and the White Lion all work, and a call ahead can hold you the upstairs or the deck.

What is the number one song played at weddings?

There isn't one. There are classics that still work for every age in the room: Celebration, September, Ain't No Mountain High Enough, Dancing Queen, Stayin' Alive, Get Down On It. We use them to open the floor, then read the room.

How much does live music add to the cost?

It depends on how many musicians, which part of the day, and how long. A DJ is $1,500. Adding sax runs $2,000 to $4,500. Pulse!, the hybrid band, starts at $3,500.

Do you handle ceremony sound if the ceremony is somewhere else?

Yes. A small-footprint satellite speaker goes wherever the ceremony is, with an omnidirectional lapel mic for the couple and a handheld for the officiant and readers. Soft-spoken vows get a second lapel.

How do you keep guests entertained between the big moments?

Music that fits the moment, not a wall of noise. Prelude for a half hour before the ceremony, cocktail sets that leave room to talk, dinner at conversation level, then the floor opens and stays open.
